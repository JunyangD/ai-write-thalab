In Guangxi, roughly one in five people carries a thalassemia trait, so transfusion-dependent cases are common in clinic. Chronic transfusion keeps loading iron into the liver, the heart, and the endocrine glands. The label "ferroaging" now ties iron overload, lipid peroxidation, and cellular senescence into one chain; whether that chain explains the early co-morbidity of TDT is still untested. As of our search date, no PubMed-indexed paper covered thalassemia and ferroaging together (pmid: N; format example, not a real citation).

The link between thalassemia and iron metabolism is well established, but where the mechanism actually lands remains contested. Non-transferrin-bound iron and the labile iron pool are the main sources of redox-active iron, and deposition is organ-selective: the liver is involved first, whereas cardiac iron directly tracks prognosis. Deferoxamine, deferiprone, and deferasirox all act on the iron load itself and leave established organ senescence untouched. That gap is the reason to bring ferroaging into the long-term management of thalassemia.
